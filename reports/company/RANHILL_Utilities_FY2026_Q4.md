## 6. Macroeconomic interpretation

### A. Johor / JS-SEZ is the most important demand-side macro catalyst
The report explicitly links higher water consumption and engineering demand to JS-SEZ and data-centre development. This creates a macro-to-company transmission mechanism:

JS-SEZ + FDI + industrial expansion
→ population/industrial activity
→ water demand
→ RanhillSAJ volume/revenue opportunity
→ water-related engineering/consultancy demand.

### B. Data centres create a second-order utility opportunity
Data centres are not only an IT investment story. They require reliable electricity, water and supporting infrastructure. Ranhill's water and engineering exposure therefore gives it indirect participation in Malaysia's data-centre capex cycle.

### C. Infrastructure policy supports the consultancy segment
The 13th Malaysia Plan emphasis on water reliability and transportation can support public infrastructure and engineering demand. This is more project-cycle dependent than the regulated/utility-like water earnings base.

### D. Energy transition changes the opportunity set
CRESS and renewable-energy development could expand Ranhill's power/engineering opportunity set. However, the current FY2026 Power segment generated only RM4.8m PBT on RM249.4m revenue, so the transition thesis has not yet translated into large earnings contribution.

### E. Interest-rate sensitivity
The group carries RM751.1m of borrowings and RM78.8m of FY2026 finance costs. Therefore, Malaysia's interest-rate environment matters to earnings and the economics of future capital expenditure. Lower financing costs would generally be supportive, while a higher-for-longer rate environment raises the hurdle rate for the RM1.299bn uncontracted capex pipeline.

### F. Government / regulatory dependency
Water infrastructure, matching grants, concession arrangements and CRESS are influenced by government policy and regulation. The RM48.9m matching-grant recognition materially lifted Q4 PBT, so investors should distinguish recurring operating earnings from policy/grant-related items.

## 7. Investment view — research framework

**Strengths**
1. Water is the dominant earnings engine.
2. Johor/JS-SEZ and data-centre development provide a credible structural demand catalyst.
3. FY2026 operating cash flow was RM511m.
4. Net assets increased to RM1.276bn.
5. Power and consultancy provide optionality from energy transition and infrastructure spending.

**Risks**
1. FY2026 Q4 has no comparable prior-quarter-year data because of the financial-year-end change.
2. Q4 PBT benefited from a RM48.9m matching grant.
3. Power profitability remains weak.
4. RM1.299bn of approved-but-not-contracted capital commitments creates execution and funding risk.
5. Debt and finance costs make the company sensitive to funding conditions.
6. Water/concession economics remain exposed to regulation and government decisions.

**Key monitoring indicators for the next 4 quarters**
- RanhillSAJ water revenue and PBT
- Johor water-consumption growth / JS-SEZ project progress
- Data-centre-related water demand and infrastructure contracts
- Consultancy order wins and margin
- Power segment turnaround
- CRESS/renewable project conversion
- Operating cash flow vs reported profit
- Net debt / finance cost
- Actual deployment of the RM1.299bn capex pipeline
- Recurring EPS excluding one-off grants

## 8. Preliminary conclusion

Ranhill Utilities is best viewed as a **water-led infrastructure/utility platform with Johor growth exposure**, rather than a pure power or renewable-energy stock.

The strongest investment thesis is:
**Water + Johor/JS-SEZ + data centres + infrastructure spending → long-duration demand growth.**

The biggest issue is not demand visibility alone; it is whether management can convert the large opportunity pipeline into recurring cash earnings without excessive capital intensity or leverage.

Overall research status: **Positive structural outlook, but require valuation and recurring-earnings verification before upgrading to a high-conviction investment candidate.**
