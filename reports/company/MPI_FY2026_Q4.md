## 5. Strategic Developments

MPI completed the acquisition of the entire issued and paid-up share capital of Infineon Technologies (Thailand) Limited on 4 February 2026 for an initial closing purchase price of USD76.938m. Following post-completion adjustment, the final purchase price was agreed at USD75.690m. The acquired entity became wholly owned and was renamed Carsem Semiconductor (Bangkok) Ltd.

This expands MPI's semiconductor manufacturing footprint and is strategically relevant to future capacity and customer opportunities.

## 6. Management Outlook

Management remains confident about benefiting from sustained growth in the global semiconductor industry.

The stated priorities are:
1. Expand into higher-margin semiconductor segments.
2. Focus particularly on AI servers and sensors.
3. Leverage technological expertise to unlock new opportunities.
4. Mitigate rising production costs amid global economic and geopolitical uncertainty.
5. Continue delivering customised solutions and strengthening long-term customer relationships.

## 7. Investment View / Future Prospects

### Overall view: Positive, but capital-intensive

MPI's FY2026 results show a clear improvement in scale and profitability. Revenue growth of 24% was accompanied by faster PBT growth of 31%, while operating profit grew about 40%. This indicates meaningful operating leverage at the full-year level.

The most attractive part of the forward story is the combination of:
- Semiconductor industry growth
- AI-server exposure
- Higher-margin segment expansion
- Strong USA growth
- New semiconductor capacity through the Thailand acquisition
- Long-term customer relationships

### Main concern

The biggest issue is cash conversion.

Operating cash flow was RM440m, while capex reached RM573m. The resulting approximate negative RM132m operating cash flow less capex means the growth cycle is consuming substantial cash.

Therefore, the next few quarters should be judged not only on revenue/PBT growth, but on whether:
higher capacity → higher-margin revenue → higher operating cash flow → improving FCF

If that chain starts to materialise, the investment thesis strengthens significantly.

## 8. Bull / Base / Bear Framework

### Bull case
AI servers, sensors and other high-value semiconductor segments grow strongly; the Thailand capacity contributes; margins improve; capex begins translating into stronger cash flow.

### Base case
Revenue continues growing with the semiconductor cycle, but rising production costs and geopolitical uncertainty limit margin expansion. Earnings continue growing, while FCF remains under pressure during the investment phase.

### Bear case
Global semiconductor demand weakens, utilisation falls, production costs remain elevated, and heavy capex delays cash-flow recovery. Earnings growth could slow materially.

## 9. What to Monitor Next

For the next 2–4 quarters, track these six indicators:
1. Revenue growth, especially USA and AI-related demand.
2. Gross / operating margin — whether higher revenue converts into stronger profitability.
3. Operating cash flow — should start recovering.
4. Capex intensity — whether spending remains elevated.
5. FCF recovery — key validation of the investment cycle.
6. Thailand acquisition contribution — capacity utilisation, revenue and margin impact.

## 10. Research Conclusion

MPI FY2026 = strong earnings growth + strong semiconductor/AI structural exposure + aggressive investment cycle.

The company looks fundamentally stronger than FY2025, but the next stage of the thesis depends on cash-flow conversion and return on the new capacity, not revenue growth alone.

For a long-term investor, MPI is worth keeping on the high-quality semiconductor / AI supply-chain watchlist, while valuation and entry price should be assessed separately using the current market price.
