## Executive summary

WPRTS delivered strong year-on-year growth in 9M2026, supported by tariff increases and stronger value-added services (VAS). Operational revenue excluding construction revenue rose 26% and profit after tax rose 42%. However, 3Q2026 operational revenue and PAT eased sequentially as unusually long-staying metal and e-waste container volumes normalised. The company is undertaking a major container terminal expansion, which brings long-term capacity opportunities alongside material capex and concession-related obligations.

### Sequential comparison: Q3 2026 vs Q2 2026

- Operational revenue: RM797.7m vs RM808.2m, down 1%.
- Gross profit: RM556.7m vs RM568.0m, down 2%.
- EBIT: RM473.3m vs RM496.6m, down 5%.
- PBT: RM450.4m vs RM471.3m, down 4%.
- PAT: RM342.6m vs RM360.9m, down 5%.

Management attributed the sequential decline mainly to lower container storage revenue following normalisation of long-staying metal and e-waste container volumes.

## Revenue quality and operating drivers

- Management said Q3 operational revenue grew 20% YoY mainly due to tariff increases and stronger VAS revenue.
- For 9M2026, operational revenue grew 26% YoY.
- Construction revenue was RM472.7m in 9M2026 versus RM198.2m in 9M2025 (+138%). It is recorded under service concession accounting for port development infrastructure and should not be treated as equivalent to recurring port operating revenue.
- The group identifies port development and port operations as its sole operating segment; activities are predominantly in Malaysia.

## Expansion, debt and dividends

- Container terminal expansion covers CT10 to CT17. Dredging and land reclamation began in 2025 and are expected to take approximately four years; CT10 is expected to be ready in 2028.
- The concession for CT10–CT13 has been extended to 31 August 2070. A further extension to 31 August 2082 for CT14–CT17 is conditional on the specified underwater-land acquisition and transfer.
- Capital expenditure commitments authorised and contracted for PPE and concession assets were RM865.9m at 30 September 2026.
- The group issued RM250m of Sukuk Wakalah Medium Term Notes in March 2026 with a 15-year tenure and redeemed RM225m of Sukuk Musharakah notes during the period.
- RAM reaffirmed AAA/Stable for SMMTN and SWMTN on 11 November 2025 (as reported in this filing).
- Dividends paid during the period included 11.92 sen per share for the FY2025 second interim dividend and 14.98 sen per share for the FY2026 first interim dividend; each included cash and dividend reinvestment plan portions. These are paid dividends reported for the period, not a forecast of future dividends.

## Management outlook and disclosed events

- Management expects total container throughput for 2026 to be at least the same as the previous year, while cautioning that fast-evolving conditions could materially affect volume.
- Management noted that the global interest-rate environment had moved upward as inflationary pressures reached markets after renewed energy-price increases, while technology investment continued to support consumption and growth optimism.
- A January 2026 vessel incident damaged three mooring dolphins and temporarily closed LBT 4 and LBT 4A jetties. They were partially reopened in February 2026; insurance claims and salvage work were ongoing. Management did not expect a material earnings impact.
- No material litigation or material contingent liabilities/assets were disclosed as at the dates specified in the report.

## Investment interpretation — not company guidance

### Positives
1. Strong 9M growth in operating revenue and profit.
2. Tariff increases and VAS contributed to operating revenue growth.
3. Robust operating cash flow and substantial liquidity.
4. Terminal expansion may add long-term capacity, subject to execution and demand.

### Risks and watch items
1. Sequential moderation in Q3 after storage revenue normalised.
2. Expansion execution, capex funding, construction timetable and future terminal utilisation.
3. Container throughput sensitivity to global trade, shipping networks, regional supply chains and macroeconomic conditions.
4. Rising total costs and the long-term burden of service concession obligations, lease liabilities and financing.
5. Construction-related revenue can inflate reported revenue growth relative to underlying port operations.
6. DRP share issuance increases the share count; use updated shares outstanding in per-share valuation.
7. The company did not provide a profit forecast or profit guarantee.
