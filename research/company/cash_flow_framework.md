# Cash Flow Analysis Framework

## Purpose and evidence discipline
Assess operating cash generation, working capital, reinvestment, financing and shareholder distributions for Bursa Malaysia companies. Profit is not cash. Use filed cash flow statements and notes.

Every material topic must be split into:
- FACT — reported or transparently calculated data, period, unit, source/page/note.
- ANALYSIS — reconciliation, trend, ratio or interpretation.
- INFERENCE — qualified implication, uncertainty and alternatives.
- FORECAST — forward estimate with horizon, assumptions and sensitivities; otherwise “No forecast made; data or assumptions are insufficient.”

Use “Data unavailable” for missing inputs. Record reporting period, comparable periods, currency, units, consolidation basis, filing date and audited/reviewed status. Keep quarter, YTD and FY values separate.

## 1. Operating cash flow bridge
Reproduce reported OCF and bridge from the filed starting profit measure. Record non-cash depreciation/amortization, impairment, fair value/disposal, provisions, FX, share payments, working capital, interest and tax as classified by the issuer. Reconcile subtotals; do not explain an aggregate “other” without note evidence.

## 2. Working capital and cash conversion
Review receivables, ageing/ECL, contract assets/liabilities, customer advances, inventory by class, payables, supplier financing, provisions, tax and other balances. Compare movements with revenue, cost of sales, project milestones, seasonality and disclosed payment terms. Cash conversion = OCF / a named profit measure only when periods and definitions match and denominator is meaningful. Separate temporary timing from recurring behavior only with evidence.

For each material movement provide:
### FACT
[Opening/closing balance, cash movement, source, period, unit.]
### ANALYSIS
[Business and period comparison; collection/stock/payment context.]
### INFERENCE
[Potential structural or temporary effect; alternatives and uncertainty.]
### FORECAST
[Explicit working-capital assumptions or no forecast.]

## 3. Investing flows, capex and free cash flow
Separate reported cash purchases of PPE/intangibles, disposals, acquisitions, investments, deposits/loans, restricted cash and other flows. Reconcile cash capex to note additions, capital creditors, leases, acquired assets and timing where possible. Distinguish maintenance/growth capex only with disclosure or label an analyst estimate.
Common analytical FCF = net operating cash flow − cash purchases of PPE and intangible assets. Define sign convention and treatment of development, acquisitions, leases, interest and other flows. Show inputs and calculation. Conventional industrial FCF may be inappropriate for financial institutions, REITs, property developers or project companies; explain and use a sector measure only when data and definition support it.

## 4. Financing, liquidity and dividends
Reconcile opening to closing cash through operating, investing and financing flows. Review borrowings, repayments, leases, interest, dividends, share issues/buybacks, cash versus restricted cash, debt maturities, facilities, rates, FX, security and covenants as disclosed. State the net-debt definition and treatment of leases/restricted cash. Never claim covenant compliance or available funding without evidence.
For dividends distinguish proposed, declared, approved, ex-date, payment date and cash paid. Record per-share and total amounts; compare with OCF, FCF, capex, debt and liquidity. Define payout denominator and disclose timing/special dividends.

## 5. Sector considerations
Banks and insurers: capital, funding and liquidity conventions may make industrial FCF unsuitable.
REITs: recurring rental cash earnings, distributions, gearing, refinancing and committed capex.
Developers/contractors: collections, billings, contract assets/liabilities, retention, claims, project commitments and timing.
Plantations/commodities: price cycles, production, seasonality, inventory and biological assets.
Manufacturing/technology: concentration, supplier terms, stock build, receivables, utilization and capex.
Use only relevant available data and explain exclusions.

## 6. Cash outlook
Separate management guidance from analyst assumptions. A forward cash scenario should disclose working-capital drivers, capex, tax/interest, repayments, distributions, commitments, period and sensitivities. Put all numbers in FORECAST. If supportable inputs are absent, say so.

## Conclusion template
### FACT
[OCF, bridge, working capital, cash capex, defined FCF, cash/debt, financing and dividend sources.]
### ANALYSIS
[Conversion, timing versus recurring movements, reinvestment and funding.]
### INFERENCE
[Cash resilience/liquidity implication, sector caveats and alternative explanations.]
### FORECAST
[Supported scenario or explicit no forecast.]

## Checklist
- [ ] Cash reconciliation and profit-to-OCF bridge.
- [ ] Receivables, contract balances, inventory and payables.
- [ ] Cash capex distinguished from balance-sheet additions.
- [ ] FCF definition/exclusions, debt, liquidity and dividends.
- [ ] Sector-specific limitations.
- [ ] Four evidence labels and unavailable-data handling.
