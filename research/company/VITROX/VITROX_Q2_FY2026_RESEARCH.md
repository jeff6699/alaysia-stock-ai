# ViTrox Corporation Berhad — Q2 FY2026 Financial Research

## 1. Executive conclusion

ViTrox delivered an exceptionally strong Q2 FY2026. Revenue more than doubled YoY and grew 40.4% QoQ, while PBT and PAT grew faster than revenue. Management attributes the improvement to AI infrastructure demand, automotive and consumer electronics, supply-chain restructuring, advanced packaging/HBM requirements, stronger shipment deliveries and customer pull-ins.

The central investment positive is not merely revenue growth: earnings are scaling faster than revenue, indicating strong operating leverage/product mix. The key caution is cash conversion. 1H PAT was RM135.3m but operating cash flow was only RM24.2m, with large working-capital absorption in receivables and inventories.

## 2. Earnings quality

### Growth
- Q2 revenue +104.8% YoY; PBT +163.7%; PAT +203.9%.
- Q2 revenue +40.4% QoQ; PBT +62.1%; PAT +66.4%.
- 1H revenue +98.0%; PBT +147.4%; PAT +161.6%.

### Margins
Approximate Q2 FY2026:
- PBT margin: 26.7%
- PAT margin: 22.5%

Approximate 1H FY2026:
- PBT margin: 25.2%
- PAT margin: 21.1%

The faster growth in PBT/PAT versus revenue is consistent with management's explanation of manufacturing scale, product mix and operational optimisation.

## 3. AI/semiconductor exposure

Management explicitly identifies AI infrastructure as a primary growth driver. The company is focusing on advanced packaging and high-precision inspection/testing for AI accelerators and HBM, while developing next-generation high-resolution machine-vision solutions.

The company states that it serves customers across more than 30 countries and provides ABI and MVS solutions.

## 4. Balance-sheet strength

Cash of RM369.0m versus total borrowings of RM82.2m gives an approximate net cash position of RM286.7m. This provides financial flexibility.

However, inventories increased from RM299.2m at 31 Dec 2025 to RM429.3m at 30 Jun 2026, while receivables increased from RM374.5m to RM527.1m. These increases require monitoring because they explain much of the weak operating cash conversion.

## 5. Cash-flow warning

1H operating cash flow was only RM24.2m versus RM135.3m PAT. Working-capital movements included:
- inventory outflow RM131.7m
- receivables outflow RM145.0m

This does not invalidate the earnings result, but it is the most important item to monitor in the next quarter: whether revenue growth converts into collections and inventory-to-cash.

## 6. Tax advantage

The effective tax rate is below the statutory 24%, mainly due to incentives enjoyed by VTSB. The exemption relates to specified advanced industrial automation systems/modules for semiconductor advanced packaging and AI Smart Factories.

This is a positive earnings support factor, but investors should distinguish operational earnings growth from tax-driven uplift.

## 7. Management outlook

Management expects a favourable 2H 2026 environment, supported by continued AI and data-centre infrastructure investment. It cites Gartner's June 2026 forecast for worldwide semiconductor revenue of US$1.56tn in 2026, +92.2% YoY, with memory revenue above US$830bn.

Management remains optimistic about automated inspection but flags FX volatility and geopolitical uncertainty as risks and intends to maintain prudent cost management.

## 8. Investment framework

### Bull case
AI/data-centre capex remains strong; HBM and advanced packaging inspection demand accelerates; shipment growth continues; operating leverage keeps margins elevated.

### Base case
AI/semiconductor demand remains healthy but normalises from the exceptional Q2 growth rate; earnings continue growing but at a slower pace; working capital gradually converts to cash.

### Bear case
AI/semiconductor investment slows, customer pull-ins reverse, inventory/receivables remain elevated, FX/geopolitical risks increase, and margins normalise.

## 9. What to monitor next

1. Q3 revenue and order/shipment momentum.
2. Operating cash flow versus PAT.
3. Receivables and inventory days/absolute balances.
4. PBT/PAT margin sustainability.
5. AI accelerator/HBM/advanced-packaging demand.
6. Impact of VTSB tax exemption as earnings scale.
7. Capex and the RM50.4m outstanding PP&E commitment.
8. FX and geopolitical exposure.
