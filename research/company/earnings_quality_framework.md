# Earnings Quality Analysis Framework

## Purpose and evidence labels
Assess persistence of reported earnings, cash backing, estimation risk, and unusual items for a Bursa Malaysia issuer. This is a diligence framework: an indicator is not proof of manipulation or misconduct.

Use four separate blocks for every material finding:
- FACT — source, page/note, date, period, units, reported values and formula.
- ANALYSIS — reconciliation, comparison, accounting interpretation and context.
- INFERENCE — qualified conclusion with uncertainty and benign alternatives.
- FORECAST — only an assumption-based forward view with horizon and sensitivity; otherwise state “No forecast made; data or assumptions are insufficient.”

Write “Data unavailable” when evidence is missing. Identify consolidated/parent basis, matched periods (quarter, comparable quarter, YTD, audited FY), currency, units, restatements and audit/review status.

## 1. Profit-to-cash conversion
Reconcile profit to operating cash flow using the filed cash flow statement. Capture non-cash depreciation/amortization, impairment, fair value and disposal items, provisions, FX, share-based payments, working-capital changes, interest and tax classification. Compare OCF with a consistently defined profit measure over several comparable periods. If using OCF / profit, name both measures and avoid the ratio where denominator is negative or mismatched. Explain seasonality, project receipts, collections and one-off timing.

## 2. Accruals and unusual earnings
List fair-value gains/losses, disposal gains, impairment/reversals, provisions/reversals, unrealized FX, tax benefits, acquisition accounting and other material items. Reconcile management’s “core” or adjusted profit to reported profit by item; check whether supposedly one-off adjustments recur and whether gains/losses are treated symmetrically. If an accrual indicator is used, define it (for example, profit less OCF divided by average assets), show inputs and limitations. Do not assume cash-flow add-backs are economically irrelevant.

## 3. Revenue, receivables and contract balances
Compare recognized revenue, receipts where disclosed, trade receivables, contract assets/liabilities, deferred income and ECL allowance. Review ageing, overdue balances, write-offs, disputes, customer concentration, related parties, progress claims, customer acceptance and subsequent collection evidence. Calculate receivable days only from comparable average balances and revenue. An increase is a signal to investigate, not a standalone adverse conclusion.

## 4. Inventory and cost recognition
Compare inventory movements with sales, cost of sales, production, utilization and seasonality. Review stock ageing, WIP, obsolescence, write-downs/reversals and inventory related to projects. Calculate inventory days only with comparable average inventory and cost of sales. Investigate margin changes alongside inventory build and cost capitalization; state what filings do and do not establish.

## 5. Capitalization, depreciation and impairment
Review PPE, intangible assets, development costs, construction in progress, useful lives, depreciation/amortization, impairment assumptions and disposals. Compare cash capex with asset additions and reconcile capital creditors, lease additions, acquisitions and timing when possible. Note policy changes and material expense capitalization only where disclosed; do not make an unsupported impairment claim.

## 6. Estimates, tax, related parties and audit
Review provisions, contingencies, warranties, contract losses, guarantees, ECL, deferred tax, incentives and prior-period tax adjustments. Capture related-party transactions and balances, loans, guarantees, terms and approval disclosures. Read the auditor’s opinion, key audit matters, emphasis of matter, going-concern uncertainty and qualifications accurately with page references. Treat governance indicators as questions, not proof of wrongdoing.

## 7. Management measures and sector context
Record management’s definition of adjusted/core profit and reconcile it to reported measures. Separate management commentary from analyst interpretation; qualitative optimism is not numerical guidance.
Adapt diagnostics: banks/insurers require asset-quality, funding and capital metrics; REITs require recurring property earnings and distribution/gearing context; developers and contractors require project receivables, contract assets/liabilities and collections; plantations/commodities require price, production, yield, inventory and biological-asset context. Explain when industrial cash-conversion or FCF ratios do not apply.

## Standard finding format
### FACT
[Reported profit, OCF, adjustments, working capital, source locator, period and units.]
### ANALYSIS
[Conversion trend, recurring versus unusual items, estimate sensitivity and sector context.]
### INFERENCE
[Calibrated view: stronger / mixed / weaker support for earnings, or insufficient evidence; include alternatives and follow-up.]
### FORECAST
[Explicit assumption-based outlook and sensitivity, or no forecast.]

## Follow-up signals (not conclusions)
Investigate persistent profit/OCF divergence; receivables, contract assets or inventory growing faster than revenue; recurring one-off adjustments; unexplained reversals; material related-party balances; large capitalization; difficult-to-reconcile impairment assumptions; or qualified/late/amended filings. For each, cite evidence, note a plausible alternative, identify the unanswered question and the next primary source needed.

## Checklist
- [ ] Matched periods, units, basis and filing status.
- [ ] Profit-to-OCF bridge and working-capital movements.
- [ ] Revenue collection, receivables, contract balances and inventory.
- [ ] Unusual items, adjusted earnings, capitalization, estimates and tax.
- [ ] Related parties and audit evidence.
- [ ] Sector caveats, uncertainty and alternative explanations.
- [ ] Four labels remain distinct; unavailable data is explicit.
