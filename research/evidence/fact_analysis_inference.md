# FACT, ANALYSIS, INFERENCE and FORECAST Rules V1.7

## Core separation
Apply one primary label to every material statement. Do not present ANALYSIS, INFERENCE, ASSUMPTION or FORECAST as FACT. If a sentence mixes claim types, split it into separate lines or label each clause.

### FACT
A claim directly supported by a traceable source. State what the source says, who reported it, when, the period, unit and locator. A company target is a FACT only as a target the company stated, not as an outcome likely to occur. A reproducible arithmetic calculation may be a calculated FACT only when all source inputs, formula, units and period are recorded; make the calculation status explicit.

### ANALYSIS
An interpretation or comparison of verified facts: trend, margin bridge, peer comparison, accounting reconciliation, or explanation of a plausible driver. Cite the facts used and disclose definitions, scope and limitations. Avoid causal certainty unless evidence demonstrates causality.

### INFERENCE
A qualified conclusion logically derived from multiple facts/analysis but not directly stated by one source. Show the supporting evidence, reasoning, alternative explanations and uncertainty. Do not write an inference in factual voice.

### FORECAST
A forward-looking estimate or prediction. Label the horizon, source facts, analyst ASSUMPTIONS, dependencies, scenario and uncertainty. Attribute management forecasts as FACT about what management guided, and as a separate FORECAST only when evaluating the future outcome. Never invent a point estimate when evidence is insufficient; use N/A or a bounded scenario only where justified.

ASSUMPTION is a tag used with analyst-selected forecast/valuation inputs; it is not evidence that the assumption will occur. It must remain identifiable in data, valuation, scenario and report records.

## Example structure (illustrative, not company data)
- FACT: The filed interim report states revenue of [source-reported value and unit] for [period], page/note [locator].
- ANALYSIS: Revenue changed versus the comparable period; this comparison uses like-for-like periods and the formula [formula].
- INFERENCE: If the mix and demand pattern persist, cash conversion may remain sensitive to receivable timing; alternative causes have not been ruled out.
- FORECAST: Under ASSUMPTION [identified case], the analyst expects [N/A unless supported] over [horizon]; this is uncertain and not company guidance.

Bracketed placeholders are not real observations and must be replaced with sourced data or N/A.

## Prohibited practices
- Converting an issuer statement, rumor, broker estimate or AI-generated text into FACT without verifying the original evidence.
- Omitting negative/contrary evidence while presenting a balanced-sounding conclusion.
- Citing only a company homepage for a precise financial value.
- Treating absence of disclosure as proof of absence.
- Mixing reporting periods, units, currencies or share bases without disclosure.
- Calling a forecast, target, estimate or analyst adjustment historical reported data.
