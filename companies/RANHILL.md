The large approved-but-not-contracted capital commitment is a key item to track: verify project scope, timing, funding source, and expected returns in future disclosures.

## 7. Management outlook stated in the report

- **Water:** Expected revenue growth supported by water consumption and developments including the Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ) and data-centre development.
- **Power:** Exploring carbon-footprint reduction and energy-transition opportunities, including participation in the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme (CRESS).
- **Consultancy and Services:** Potential opportunities related to the 13th Malaysia Plan, water reliability, water treatment/distribution, non-revenue water solutions, sewerage treatment, JS-SEZ, data-centre-related water demand, solar and hydropower engineering.

These are management expectations, not guaranteed outcomes.

## 8. Investor assessment — preliminary, not a valuation

**Potential positives**
1. Water is the core earnings engine and provides exposure to essential utility demand.
2. Net assets rose to RM1.276b and reported cash/deposits increased compared with 30 June 2025.
3. Operating cash flow was positive at RM511.4m.
4. JS-SEZ and data-centre development may support water demand and consultancy opportunities, as described by management.

**Key risks / diligence items**
1. No comparable prior-period income statement and cash-flow data due to the fiscal year-end change; establish a comparable historical trend from annual reports and future quarters.
2. Q4 PBT benefited from a RM48.9m matching grant; assess normalised earnings excluding non-recurring support.
3. RM113.9m expected-credit-loss allowance for the year and material receivables warrant review of collection and ageing.
4. Power segment earned only RM4.8m PBT for the year and made a Q4 loss of RM8.0m.
5. RM1.314b capital commitments, mainly approved but not contracted, could affect future funding needs.
6. Borrowings were RM751.1m and include secured bonds; RM300.9m of subsidiary borrowings are covered by corporate guarantees from the Company.
7. Large concession-asset amortisation and PAAB lease-rental payments make cash-flow and concession accounting important to understand.

**Preliminary stance:** Maintain on the research/watch list pending comparable historical results, details of capital commitments, receivable ageing/collections, and normalised earnings. This report alone does not support a buy/sell conclusion or a fair-value estimate.
