## Investment summary
RANHILL is a water utilities, power and engineering/consultancy group. Water is the principal earnings contributor. Management expects water demand and consultancy opportunities to benefit from Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ), industrial/residential development and data-centre activity. Key watch items are power-segment profitability, expected-credit-loss allowances, concession amortisation, and a large amount of approved but uncontracted capital commitments.

## Management outlook stated in the report
1. **Water:** Expected revenue growth supported by water consumption and catalytic development including JS-SEZ and data-centre development.
2. **Power:** Exploring carbon-footprint reduction and energy-transition opportunities, including participation in the Corporate Renewable Energy Supply Scheme (CRESS).
3. **Consultancy and services:** Potential opportunities related to the 13th Malaysia Plan, water reliability, treatment/distribution, non-revenue water solutions, sewerage treatment, JS-SEZ and renewable-energy engineering.

## Investor assessment (analysis, not company guidance)
**Positives**
- Water business provides a clear core earnings base.
- Positive operating cash flow and improved net current asset position.
- Potential structural demand from Johor development, industrial activity and data centres.

**Risks / questions**
- No comparable prior-year income statement in this report, limiting trend analysis.
- Q4 PBT was helped by a RM48.9m matching grant; sustainability of underlying earnings needs verification.
- High expected-credit-loss allowance (RM113.9m FY2026).
- Power segment remains low-margin and volatile.
- RM1.299bn of approved but uncontracted capex could create execution and financing demands.
- Large service-concession amortisation affects reported earnings; assess cash generation alongside accounting profit.

## Follow-up checklist
- Compare the next report with this FY2026 baseline and seek like-for-like trends.

- Check which approved capital commitments become contracted and how they are funded.
- Review dividends, debt maturities and operating cash flow.
