## 4. Cash flow

For the nine months ended 30 September 2026:

- Net cash from/(used in) operating activities: **-RM164.696m**, versus +RM2.770m in 9M FY2025.
- Increase in inventories used RM55.165m of cash.
- Increase in receivables and contract assets used RM127.486m of cash.
- Net cash used in investing activities: -RM22.613m.
- Net cash from financing activities: +RM143.564m, including a RM161.139m net increase in short-term borrowings and RM10.830m term-loan drawdowns.
- Cash and cash equivalents at period end: RM55.219m.

**Key concern:** Accounting profit has not translated into operating cash generation during the period. Growth may be absorbing significant working capital and has been supported by financing inflows. Future reports should show whether receivables convert into cash and borrowings stabilise.

## 6. Management outlook

Management says the wire and cable industry is supported by infrastructure, utilities, construction, telecommunications, renewable energy, data centres and industrial activity, while noting global economic/geopolitical uncertainty, cost pressures and competition. The Group plans to focus on cable manufacturing/distribution and engineering/telecommunication infrastructure work, while investing in production capacity, efficiency and quality. Outlook is described as cautiously optimistic, barring unforeseen circumstances.

## 7. Investment research assessment (based only on this report)

**Positives**
- Strong revenue growth across all three reportable segments.
- Q3 profit improved sequentially and year on year.
- Exposure to cables and infrastructure-related demand, including data centres and renewable energy.

**Risks / items to verify**
- Negative operating cash flow and major working-capital absorption.
- Borrowings increased to RM262.541m; a large share is current.
- 9M gross margin and PBT margin were below prior-year levels.
- Higher effective tax and interest costs weighed on conversion of revenue growth into attributable earnings.

**Provisional view:** Operating momentum is strong, but cash conversion and balance-sheet risk are material. Do not classify as a clear-value opportunity from earnings growth alone; valuation and entry-price analysis requires the current share price, market capitalisation, forward earnings assumptions and comparable-company multiples.

## 8. Follow-up checklist

1. Check next quarter's operating cash flow, receivables ageing and contract-asset collection.
2. Track total/current borrowings, finance costs and available banking facilities.
3. Check gross margin and attributable profit growth against revenue growth.
4. Verify order book, capacity utilisation and actual revenue/profit contribution from data-centre, renewable-energy and BESS-related demand.
5. Update valuation scenarios and margin of safety using a verified market price.
