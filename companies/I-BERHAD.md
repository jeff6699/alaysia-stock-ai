# I-Berhad (Bursa Malaysia: 0299) — Q2 FY2026 / 1H FY2026

> Source: Unaudited condensed consolidated interim financial report for the quarter and six months ended 30 June 2026, authorised for issue on 6 August 2026. All figures in RM unless otherwise stated.

## Executive summary
- 1H FY2026 revenue: RM92.461m, down 25.6% YoY from RM124.352m.
- 1H FY2026 profit before tax (PBT): RM27.048m, up 1.6% YoY from RM26.619m.
- 1H FY2026 profit attributable to owners: RM22.053m, up 3.0% YoY from RM21.409m.
- 1H EPS: 1.19 sen vs 1.15 sen.
- Key contrast: revenue fell sharply, mainly reflecting weaker property development and slightly softer leisure/hospitality revenue, while investment property earnings and property development PBT improved.
- Ordinary-share interim dividend: none recommended for the quarter.

### Segment observations
- **Property Development:** PBT increased to RM12.1m from RM10.3m, attributed by management mainly to the higher-margin sales of 8 Premier and Twenty8. Revenue fell due to lower recognised sales.
- **Property Investment:** strongest recurring earnings contributor. PBT rose about 29% to RM13.0m. Management cited approximately 96% occupancy at Mercu Maybank Corporate Tower, full occupancy at Block M Data Centre, and contributions from Central i-City Mall (40% interest).
- **Leisure & Hospitality:** PBT fell to RM2.9m from RM7.7m, mainly due to softer visitor spending. Management said the hospitality business returned to profitability in 1H FY2026 compared with a loss in the prior-year period.
- **Quarter-on-quarter:** Q2 revenue rose 10% to RM48.5m from RM43.9m and PBT rose 13% to RM14.4m from RM12.7m, helped by seasonal school/public holiday demand and higher property development earnings.

## Management outlook
- Management cites approximately RM5 billion remaining GDV as the development pipeline.
- Strategy combines property development, recurring investment-property income, leisure/hospitality, and AI World / AI Living initiatives.
- AI World and AI Living are positioned as future growth and differentiation platforms; their eventual financial contribution remains to be demonstrated.
- Management aims to strengthen recurring earnings, monetise the development pipeline progressively, improve operating-business returns, and advance AI-related initiatives.

## Investment interpretation (analysis, not company guidance)
### Positives
1. PBT and attributable profit grew slightly despite the substantial revenue decline.
2. Investment property PBT increased 29% and provides a recurring-earnings base.
3. Property development PBT improved despite lower revenue, suggesting a more favourable sales/margin mix in the period.
4. Total liabilities declined from year-end 2025 and net assets per share edged up.

### Risks / items to monitor
1. Revenue contracted 25.6% in 1H, led by a 55% decline in property development revenue.
2. Leisure & Hospitality PBT dropped 62%, with management pointing to softer visitor spending.
3. Operating cash flow remained negative at RM18.6m; cash and bank balances were RM9.2m at period end, alongside RM22.8m of short-term deposits.
4. RCULS liability component of RM203.2m and total borrowings require monitoring, including future coupon/cash obligations and potential conversion terms.
5. Remaining GDV and AI initiatives are opportunities, not guaranteed future earnings; execution and monetisation are key.
6. No ordinary-share interim dividend was recommended for the quarter.

## Monitoring checklist for next results
- Property development sales recognition, new sales and margins.
- Investment property occupancy, rental growth and data-centre contribution.
- Visitor spending, leisure margins and hotel profitability.
- Operating cash flow conversion and liquidity.
- RCULS balance, coupon payments, repayment/conversion developments.
- Concrete milestones, capex, customer uptake and earnings contribution from AI World / AI Living.
