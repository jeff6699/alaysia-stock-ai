# Keyfield International Berhad (KEYFIELD, 5321) — Q3 FY2026

- **Source:** Interim Financial Report, third quarter ended 30 September 2026 (unaudited), 19 pages.
- **Report period:** Q3 and nine months ended 30 September 2026.
- **Business:** Offshore vessel chartering and related onboard services, including accommodation work boats (AWBs) and anchor handling tug supply vessels (AHTS).
- **Archive date:** 14 November 2026.

## 1. Executive summary

Q3 revenue rebounded strongly, rising 30.4% year on year to RM172.472m and 51.9% quarter on quarter. However, gross profit fell 9.8% year on year and profit before tax fell 7.6%; PAT attributable to owners was almost flat at RM41.395m. The main issue is lower profitability per charter day and a larger contribution from lower-margin AHTS/third-party vessel activity. Nine-month revenue and profit remain below the prior year, while reported other income includes a significant disposal gain. Operating cash flow was negative due largely to a sharp increase in receivables.

## 3. Operating performance and drivers

- Own-vessel revenue in Q3 was RM119.656m, up from RM111.215m; third-party vessel revenue was RM52.816m, up from RM21.130m.
- Own-vessel utilisation improved to 89.0% (1,211 chartered days) from 81.4% (974 days) a year earlier.
- Third-party chartered days rose to 662 from 305, with five additional third-party vessels; management noted stronger AHTS demand.
- Gross profit was pressured by a higher mix of AHTS, which earn less gross profit per charter day than AWBs; moderated AWB charter rates; a strategy to lower third-party gross profit per charter day to gain market share; and higher own-vessel depreciation (RM17.6m in Q3 versus RM12.4m).
- Quarter-on-quarter, revenue rose 51.9%, gross profit 82.0%, PBT 115.6% and PATAMI 79.3%, supported by vessels commencing charters after the monsoon season and fewer off-hire costs.

## 5. Capital expenditure, fleet and dividends

- 9M PPE additions were RM141.4m: RM56.5m for one new DP2 accommodation work boat and two new 90MT DP2 AHTS vessels; RM30.1m for Keyfield Joyful; and RM54.8m for vessel equipment, special-survey dry-docking and other items.
- Contracted capital commitments at 30 September were RM263.644m versus RM81.242m a year earlier. This included RM163.6m remaining for newbuilds and RM90.6m for Keyfield Karuna; the report states Keyfield Karuna was fully paid for and delivered on 15 October 2026.
- A 2.0 sen Q3 interim dividend was declared on 12 November 2026, payable 8 January 2027 to shareholders on the register as at 23 December 2026.
- Total dividends declared for FY2026 to date were 6.5 sen per share, including a 2.0 sen special dividend, with a reported payout ratio of 43.5% of 9M PATAMI.

## 6. Outlook and strategic developments

Management stated that demand remained robust in Malaysia, the Middle East and Thailand and expected year-on-year improvement in utilisation in coming quarters. All five vessels deployed to the Middle East were reported on-hire. The company expects its fleet renewal and three newbuild vessels to support offshore activity in 2027–2028.

Keyfield Karuna, a 32,205 m³ trailing suction hopper dredger, was delivered in October 2026. Reactivation work is in progress, with commercial operations targeted for 2027. Management's longer-term objective is to increase non-oil-and-gas contribution to up to 20% of total revenue/profitability.

Seasonality matters: the company says monsoon conditions in the South China Sea, typically December to March, tend to reduce chartering activity or rates in Q1 and Q4.

## 7. Risks and watchlist

1. **Margin compression:** revenue growth has not translated into gross-profit growth; track gross margin and average daily charter rates.
2. **Cash conversion/receivables:** receivables rose sharply and 9M operating cash flow was negative.
3. **Seasonality:** Q4 may be weaker due to the monsoon season.
4. **Capital commitments:** ongoing newbuilds and vessel acquisitions require substantial cash.
5. **Oil-and-gas exposure:** demand depends on offshore activity, global oil prices, energy-security priorities, geopolitical events and weather.
6. **Middle East operational/security risk:** management reported additional war-risk insurance and monitoring.
7. **Litigation:** a subsidiary is defending claims brought by MVS Marine Services FZCO; procedural applications were filed in October 2026. Monitor subsequent disclosures and case progress.
8. **Execution risk for diversification:** Keyfield Karuna must complete reactivation and begin commercial operations as planned.
9. **Disposal-related earnings:** separate disposal gains from recurring vessel-chartering earnings when assessing valuation.

## 8. Investment framework — preliminary, not a price target

- **Business quality:** established offshore vessel-chartering platform, with utilisation recovery and international deployment.
- **Growth drivers:** AHTS demand, higher utilisation, fleet additions, Middle East contracts and potential dredging revenue.
- **Main concern:** earnings quality and cash conversion; 9M gross profit fell sharply despite a large disposal gain boosting other income.
- **Valuation discipline:** do not derive a fair value from headline 9M PBT alone. Normalise earnings by separating disposal gains, assess sustainable charter rates/utilisation and depreciation, and verify the latest market price and share count before calculating PE, EV/EBITDA or margin of safety.
- **Monitoring checklist for next results:** receivables collection; operating cash flow; gross margin; own-vessel utilisation and daily charter rates; charter renewal visibility; cash balance and debt; capex paid versus commitments; Keyfield Karuna operational start; litigation updates; dividends.
