# Score Interpretation V1

## Score bands

| Score | Grade | Reading |
|---:|---|---|
| 90–100 | Exceptional | Broadly strong evidence across the rubric; verify that valuation, risks and data quality support the result. |
| 80–89 | Strong | Attractive combination of scored strengths; examine weak metrics, concentration and downside scenarios. |
| 70–79 | Good | More strengths than weaknesses, with material trade-offs to assess. |
| 60–69 | Average / Watch | Mixed profile; identify what must improve or what price/risk condition would change the view. |
| 50–59 | Weak | Several important weaknesses or limited support; require further diligence. |
| Below 50 | High Risk / Avoid or Investigate Further | Significant weaknesses under this rubric. “Avoid” is a research label, not an instruction or automatic trade decision. |

These are standardized descriptive bands, not calibrated return probabilities, target prices, or buy/sell signals. Do not compare scores across versions unless weights, thresholds and sector treatment are comparable.

## Coverage and N/A
Show earned points, available points, coverage, and all N/A metrics.
- Full coverage: TOTAL SCORE = earned points / 100; assign the listed grade.
- Partial coverage: TOTAL SCORE = earned / available (coverage x%). Do not silently treat N/A as zero or quietly erase its weight.
- If coverage is at least 80%, an optional normalized comparison is earned / available × 100. Label it “coverage-normalized, provisional”; retain the unnormalized total beside it. Only show its provisional band if clearly labeled.
- Below 80% coverage, withhold a grade. With partial coverage, standard Investment Grade is “Provisional — insufficient coverage for a standard grade,” even if the normalized score is high.
- A valid poor observation scores zero and remains available. N/A is only for absent, invalid, or genuinely inapplicable measures with explanation.

## Confidence label
Report High / Medium / Low confidence separately from score:
- High: ≥90% coverage, recent comparable source data, strong citation trail, meaningful peer context, no major unresolved restatement or definition issue.
- Medium: 80–<90% coverage or a material but bounded comparability/peer limitation.
- Low: <80% coverage, stale or unaudited inputs, major definition issues, sparse peer evidence, or material unresolved risk. Any one severe data limitation may lower confidence.

Confidence describes evidence quality, not investment attractiveness.

## High-risk flags and interpretation overrides
A numeric score cannot cancel a severe issue. Show a separate flag and the relevant source if any apply:
- going-concern uncertainty, qualified audit opinion, material unremedied regulatory/listing issue, or credible evidence of financial distress;
- negative equity, severe liquidity/refinancing risk, or imminent maturity with no evidenced funding plan;
- suspected data integrity, material unresolved restatement, or unavailable critical financial statements;
- material unresolved governance, related-party, legal, or safety concern.
Do not assert these flags without primary evidence. A flag does not automatically change arithmetic; it requires explicit discussion and may withhold the Investment View pending diligence.

## Interpretation workflow
1. Confirm score version, date, ticker, sector, benchmark, periods, price date and data sources.
2. Check total points possible and coverage before interpreting grade.
3. Identify strongest and weakest metric scores, explaining evidence and sector context.
4. Inspect whether high valuation, technical or industry points obscure poor cash conversion, leverage or moat evidence.
5. Review risks, catalysts, and Bull/Base/Bear scenarios. Forecasts are assumptions, not facts.
6. State data gaps and confidence separately.
7. Provide an Investment View as a bounded research conclusion; do not convert grade into an automatic buy/sell recommendation.

## FACT / ANALYSIS / INFERENCE / FORECAST
- FACT: sourced metric, reporting period, price date, unit, calculation, coverage and score mapping.
- ANALYSIS: how inputs map to rubric; peer/sector adjustments, strengths, weaknesses, warnings.
- INFERENCE: overall implications, uncertainty, counter-evidence and conditions that could change the view.
- FORECAST: explicit forward scenarios and assumptions only; do not imply a forecast from historical score alone.

## Limitations
The score is sensitive to chosen periods, data quality, peer group, sector definitions and changing market prices. Technical measures are time-sensitive. Relative valuation and industry forecasts can be unreliable where peers or sources are weak. The framework is a decision-support tool for further research, not personalized financial advice or a recommendation to buy, sell, or hold.
