# AI Moat Scoring Rules V1

## Shared evidence and scoring anchors
Use evidence from filings, contracts disclosed by the issuer, audited operating results, customer/supplier evidence, product documentation, registries, regulator notices, and credible industry sources. Cite publisher, date, page/URL, period and company/segment. Company assertions are FACT only as statements made by management; their truth and results require corroboration.

Keep FACT, ANALYSIS, INFERENCE and FORECAST separate. For every relevant dimension capture measure, evidence, 0–10 score, weak signal, warning signal, strength signal, path to improve, and path to deteriorate. Use N/R only if the business model does not rely on the dimension; explain why. Use N/A when relevant evidence is missing/unreliable. No-evidence is not proof of a moat and does not earn points.

General ordinal anchors, applied with the dimension-specific criteria below:
- 0–2: no demonstrated advantage, absent, or eroding; very weak evidence.
- 3–4: early, narrow or readily matched advantage.
- 5–6: established but mixed, limited in scope or durability.
- 7–8: strong, economically evidenced advantage with meaningful barriers.
- 9–10: exceptional, sustained, independently corroborated and difficult to replicate.
Award only what evidence supports; explain judgment for intermediate scores. These are evidence-strength anchors, not claimed predictive probabilities. Do not average marketing claims into a high score.

## 1. Brand Power — 10
- Measures: customer preference, trust, pricing resilience, repeat purchase, and reputation advantage attributable to brand.
- Evidence required: multi-year price/mix, repeat purchase/retention or share evidence; customer surveys or independently sourced recognition; brand-related economics and peers where available.
- Scoring: 0–2 brand is unknown/negative or no evidence; 3–4 recognition exists but limited preference/pricing; 5–6 repeat demand or some premium, mixed durability; 7–8 sustained loyalty/premium across cycles; 9–10 independently evidenced durable preference and pricing power across key markets.
- Weak signals: high awareness but no price, retention or demand evidence.
- Warning signals: persistent discounting, declining repeat demand, reputational damage, product commoditization.
- Strength signals: resilient price/mix, low churn, organic repeat demand, strong customer trust.
- Improve: consistency, service recovery, product quality, local trust and demonstrable customer value.
- Deteriorate: quality failures, mis-selling, poor service, reputational harm, competitors with superior value.
- Data quality: distinguish management brand claims from independent evidence; market share alone does not establish brand strength.

## 2. Cost Advantage — 10
- Measures: sustainable ability to deliver comparable value at structurally lower unit cost.
- Evidence required: multi-year unit costs, margins, yields, procurement, utilization, logistics, energy or productivity data and comparable peers.
- Scoring: 0–2 higher/unknown unit cost; 3–4 isolated or temporary cost benefit; 5–6 demonstrated advantage but cycle-sensitive; 7–8 repeatable lower cost supported by process/location/procurement; 9–10 sustained peer-leading cost over a full cycle with evidence of defensibility.
- Weak signals: current high margins with no cost breakdown.
- Warning signals: input/energy wage exposure, low utilization, subsidy dependence, single source, FX shocks, obsolete plant.
- Strength signals: lower delivered unit cost, resilient margins through downturn, productivity/yield improvement.
- Improve: automation, process redesign, procurement scale, energy efficiency, waste reduction.
- Deteriorate: cost inflation, underinvestment, low utilization, loss of supplier terms, competitors copying process.
- Data quality: normalize product mix, geography, accounting and cycle; gross margin is only a proxy.

## 3. Scale Advantage — 10
- Measures: benefit from size in unit economics, coverage, purchasing, learning, capacity or investment.
- Evidence required: output/cost curves, network/asset utilization, procurement terms, geographic reach, peer comparisons and incremental return on expansion.
- Scoring: 0–2 scale adds no evidenced benefit; 3–4 size is present but inefficient; 5–6 some cost/reach benefits offset by complexity; 7–8 efficient scale advantages and reinvestment capacity; 9–10 large, durable and hard-to-match economics with disciplined returns.
- Weak signals: headline revenue/assets without lower costs or returns.
- Warning signals: diseconomies, idle capacity, poor acquisition integration, concentration or capital misallocation.
- Strength signals: improving unit economics, utilization, buying power, coverage and returns with scale.
- Improve: fill capacity, standardize operations, expand adjacent markets with proven economics.
- Deteriorate: overexpansion, bureaucracy, fixed-cost burden, fragmentation or scale leapfrogged by peers.
- Data quality: compare like business mix, not absolute company size alone.

## 4. Switching Costs — 10
- Measures: customer cost, risk, effort or disruption in changing supplier/system.
- Evidence required: retention/churn, contract renewal, implementation/integration, training, downtime, migration and multi-vendor disclosures.
- Scoring: 0–2 customers can readily switch/no evidence; 3–4 modest friction or short contracts; 5–6 meaningful process/integration but tested alternatives; 7–8 high documented retention and migration cost; 9–10 embedded mission-critical use with long demonstrated retention and low feasible substitution.
- Weak signals: long contracts without renewal or churn evidence.
- Warning signals: open standards, easy data export, customer concentration, contract expirations, credible cheaper substitutes.
- Strength signals: renewal, high adoption, workflow integration, low churn and expanding use.
- Improve: reliability, service, interoperability that adds value, workflow embedding and customer outcomes.
- Deteriorate: outages, poor support, lock-in backlash, easy migration, regulation or open-source replacement.
- Data quality: do not infer lock-in merely from switching inconvenience or unverified retention claims.

## 5. Network Effects — 10
- Measures: product/service value rises for each user, partner, developer, merchant or participant as the network grows.
- Evidence required: active users on both sides, engagement, match quality, liquidity, retention, contribution density, geographic coverage and evidence of positive feedback.
- Scoring: 0–2 no feedback mechanism; 3–4 network claimed but limited activity; 5–6 local or narrow effects with mixed growth; 7–8 evidenced cross-side value and retention; 9–10 durable self-reinforcing network with strong density and defensibility.
- Weak signals: total registrations/downloads without active use or interaction.
- Warning signals: multi-homing, low engagement, fraud, poor match quality, disintermediation, regulatory caps.
- Strength signals: increasing activity improves outcomes and retention for multiple sides.
- Improve: solve liquidity/density bottlenecks, trust/safety, participant tools and interoperability.
- Deteriorate: engagement loss, fragmentation, take-rate pressure, trust failure or competitor network migration.
- Data quality: define active participants and period; network size alone is not a network effect.

## 6. Distribution Advantage — 10
- Measures: lower-cost, exclusive, trusted or hard-to-replicate access to customers/markets.
- Evidence required: channel coverage, acquisition/fulfilment cost, conversion, exclusivity/contract terms, customer reach and channel economics.
- Scoring: 0–2 no advantage; 3–4 ordinary channels easily matched; 5–6 useful but partly dependent on third parties; 7–8 broad or privileged access with proven economics; 9–10 difficult-to-replicate distribution and consistently superior reach/cost.
- Weak signals: number of outlets/dealers without productivity or access proof.
- Warning signals: platform disintermediation, channel conflict, expiring exclusivity, high commissions, single distributor.
- Strength signals: efficient customer acquisition, repeat channel sales, hard-to-match service/logistics coverage.
- Improve: strengthen partner economics, route density, omnichannel service and local market access.
- Deteriorate: channel concentration, contract loss, platform shift, logistics cost or regulatory limits.
- Data quality: distinguish owned reach from third-party dependence; do not assume Malaysian coverage from a few outlets.

## 7. Technology / IP — 10
- Measures: proprietary technology, patents, trade secrets, process know-how, licenses or product capability that creates differentiated value.
- Evidence required: granted patent/registered rights or documented know-how, product performance, adoption, renewal/licensing revenue, R&D outcomes and replication barriers.
- Scoring: 0–2 no differentiated technology evidence; 3–4 feature or IP portfolio with unclear commercial impact; 5–6 differentiated products with some adoption but uncertain defensibility; 7–8 protected/complex technology with commercial results; 9–10 independently corroborated, hard-to-reproduce technology with sustained economic value.
- Weak signals: patent counts or R&D spend without products, adoption or protection.
- Warning signals: expiry, infringement, obsolescence, open-source substitute, talent loss, freedom-to-operate dispute.
- Strength signals: paid adoption, renewal, superior performance, licensing or validated process advantage.
- Improve: focused R&D, commercialization, protection, talent and integration with customer workflows.
- Deteriorate: weak commercialization, imitation, rights expiry, technical debt or legal challenge.
- Data quality: verify status/scope in relevant registry (including MyIPO when applicable); patent existence is not proof of moat.

## 8. Data Advantage — 10
- Measures: lawful, differentiated, high-quality and hard-to-recreate data that improves product, operations or decisions.
- Evidence required: provenance/rights, coverage, freshness, uniqueness, scale, quality, model/product lift, feedback loops and privacy/security controls.
- Scoring: 0–2 no differentiated lawful data; 3–4 data exists but is generic/fragmented; 5–6 useful dataset with uncertain uniqueness or impact; 7–8 differentiated data with measurable product/operating lift and governance; 9–10 compounding, hard-to-recreate and lawfully used data advantage with durable outcomes.
- Weak signals: large data volume without rights, quality or use evidence.
- Warning signals: consent/privacy breach, stale/biased data, portability, commoditized sources, weak security.
- Strength signals: measurable accuracy/service/cost gains, proprietary workflow capture, strong governance.
- Improve: lawful collection, quality, structure, consent, security, feedback and outcome measurement.
- Deteriorate: loss of access/consent, breach, bias, regulation, customer portability or data commoditization.
- Data quality: never assume access rights. Do not request or expose personal/sensitive data; cite only public/disclosed evidence.

## 9. AI Capability — 10
- Measures: ability to select, build, deploy, govern and maintain AI in products/processes with repeatable results.
- Evidence required: deployed use cases, adoption, outcome/KPI changes, reliability, model/data governance, costs, talent, human oversight and post-deployment monitoring.
- Scoring: 0–2 no credible capability or unsafe/failed deployment; 3–4 pilots/claims without outcomes; 5–6 one or more deployed uses with partial evidence; 7–8 repeatable deployments with measured benefit, governance and adoption; 9–10 portfolio-scale capability, sustained outcome evidence, reliable governance and learning advantage.
- Weak signals: AI branding, demos or spend without production adoption.
- Warning signals: hallucinations/errors, model dependence, cost overruns, IP/privacy breaches, poor controls, vendor lock-in.
- Strength signals: audited/credible productivity or customer outcomes, safe deployment, reuse and continued adoption.
- Improve: target measurable use cases, secure data rights, human review, controls, training and unit economics.
- Deteriorate: failures, talent attrition, rapidly commoditized features, vendors capturing value, governance lapse.
- Data quality: distinguish announced pilots from production use; management claims remain claims until outcome evidence is available.

## 10. Capital / Ecosystem Advantage — 10
- Measures: privileged financing, long-duration investment capacity, partner ecosystem, complementary assets, supplier/customer coordination or regulatory capital that enables superior competition.
- Evidence required: cost/access/duration of capital, partnership terms, ecosystem participation, complementary assets, reinvestment outcomes and comparable funding position.
- Scoring: 0–2 no advantage or constrained capital; 3–4 ordinary access/loose partnerships; 5–6 useful ecosystem or capital access with mixed exclusivity/economics; 7–8 durable partnerships/assets and ability to reinvest through cycles; 9–10 hard-to-replicate ecosystem/capital position with compounding, verified outcomes.
- Weak signals: partnership announcements or cash balances without strategic outcomes.
- Warning signals: dependence on one funder/partner, refinancing risk, capital misallocation, partner conflicts or subsidy withdrawal.
- Strength signals: repeated attractive investment, reciprocal partner value, resilience through cycles and complementary reach.
- Improve: disciplined reinvestment, deeper reciprocal integration, funding diversity and partner alignment.
- Deteriorate: capital scarcity, partner exit, debt stress, ecosystem bypass or poor returns on investment.
- Data quality: distinguish binding contracts from memoranda and cash from committed financing.

## Scoring and trajectory data record
For each dimension record stable ID, relevance (relevant/N_R), evidence status (scored/N_A), score 0–10, current facts, sources/dates, weak/warning/strength signals, improvement/deterioration path, confidence, Year +1/+2/+3 outlook and trajectory rating. Dimensions have equal 10-point weights only among relevant, scoreable dimensions; disclose the denominator and coverage. Do not silently substitute a different moat category.
