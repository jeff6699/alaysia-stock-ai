# AI Moat Evolution Framework — V1

## Purpose and time horizon
Use a rolling 3-year base to assess Year 0/current moat, expected Year +1, Year +2 and Year +3 direction. Separate observed evidence from analyst scenarios. Revisit at each reporting cycle and preserve dated prior assessments for comparison.

For every dimension and overall conclusion, use:
- FACT — sourced current/history evidence, date, period and locator.
- ANALYSIS — comparison and causal reasoning.
- INFERENCE — qualified interpretation, uncertainty and alternative outcomes.
- FORECAST — expected Year +1/+2/+3 path, assumptions, dependencies and falsifiers.

## Year 0 to Year +3 assessment
For each relevant dimension show:
| Dimension | Year 0/current FACT and score | Year +1 FORECAST | Year +2 FORECAST | Year +3 FORECAST | 3-year trajectory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Trajectory codes:
- +2 = Strongly strengthening
- +1 = Strengthening
- 0 = Stable
- −1 = Weakening
- −2 = Rapidly deteriorating

Assign the trajectory code to the dimension’s expected net direction over the full 3-year horizon, not simply its first-year change. Explain the path year by year. A positive direction requires a plausible causal mechanism and observable evidence; an announced plan alone is not delivery evidence. Use N/A when evidence cannot support a direction and N/R for a dimension not relevant to the business.

## Current Moat Score
Score applicable dimensions using ai_moat_scoring_rules.md. Current Moat Score = earned points / scoreable relevant maximum ×100. Also report total relevant points, scoreable relevant points and coverage = scoreable relevant / relevant. N/R dimensions are excluded with reason; N/A relevant dimensions reduce coverage. A low but evidenced moat score is zero or low, never N/A. If evidence coverage is below 70%, report Current Moat Score as N/A for composite calculation; a preliminary normalized observation may be shown only as unweighted/provisional.

## Overall AI Moat Evolution Score
Five components add to exactly 100:

| Component | Formula | Max |
|---|---|---:|
| Current moat strength | Current Moat Score × 0.30 | 30 |
| Moat durability | Durability rubric points | 20 |
| 3-year trajectory | 5 × (mean dimension trajectory + 2) | 20 |
| AI disruption resilience | Inverse-risk points under risk rubric | 15 |
| AI opportunity | Opportunity points under opportunity rubric | 15 |
| TOTAL | Sum of available weighted component points | 100 |

Trajectory mean is the arithmetic mean of the scored relevant dimensions’ −2…+2 codes; round only the final component to one decimal. Trajectory evidence coverage = dimensions with a supported trajectory / relevant dimensions; if below 70%, mark the 3-year trajectory component N/A. Equal dimension weights avoid unsupported importance precision; explain a justified sector variant in a later version, not by silently changing V1.
AI disruption resilience uses 15 points for the least residual risk and 0 for the most severe. In the report, show both the points and residual risk label so the inverse direction is clear.
AI opportunity is incremental, credible potential to reinforce moat; do not double count the current AI Capability dimension.
If any component is N/A, composite = earned / available and coverage = available /100. Do not call N/A zero or renormalize away. Optional normalized score = earned / available ×100 only at ≥80% coverage, labeled provisional. Below 80%, overall score/rating is N/A — insufficient evidence. A final formal rating requires ≥80% coverage; label it provisional if coverage is below 100%.

## Durability rubric — 20 points
Score five factors 0–4 each:
1. Barrier to replication and time/capital/know-how required.
2. Historical evidence of customer preference, retention, margins, cost or returns through a cycle.
3. Exposure to substitutes, new entrants, commoditization and regulation.
4. Ability to reinvest, refresh IP/product/channel and adapt to change.
5. Breadth and independence of moat sources; concentration and single-point failure.
For each factor: 0 = severe weakness/no support; 1 = weak; 2 = mixed; 3 = strong; 4 = unusually strong and corroborated. Cite facts, explain score, and keep forecast durability separate. If a factor is unevaluable, mark it N/A and report available points. Durability evidence coverage = available factor points /20. At ≥70%, normalize earned / available to the /20 composite component; below 70%, the component is N/A.

## AI disruption risk and resilience rubric — 15 points
Assess the probability/exposure and consequence of AI or AI-enabled entrants changing customer value, economics, barriers, or value capture. Score resilience points (higher is better):
- 13–15: Low residual risk; core differentiation is hard to automate/copy or the company has demonstrated adaptation, governance and protected economics.
- 10–12: Manageable risk; exposure exists but response, customer value and economics have credible evidence.
- 7–9: Moderate risk; meaningful exposure, mixed response evidence or uncertainty.
- 4–6: High risk; AI could erode important economics and response is early/unproven.
- 0–3: Severe risk; core value is readily substituted, economics already erode, and no credible mitigation is evidenced.
Rate residual AI Disruption Risk separately as Low / Moderate / High / Unknown. Review exposure, value-chain shifts, model/provider dependence, data rights, privacy, cyber, regulation, costs, incumbent advantage, open models, AI-native entrants and speed of adoption. Do not equate AI adoption announcements with resilience.

## AI Opportunity rubric — 15 points
Measure ability to use AI to materially strengthen or extend an existing or prospective moat, above current AI capability. Score:
- 13–15: clear, near/medium-term use cases linked to durable economics, lawful differentiated inputs, funded execution, adoption evidence/KPIs and defensible capture of value.
- 10–12: credible use cases, management ownership/funding, measurable milestones and plausible moat reinforcement; some dependencies remain.
- 7–9: relevant use cases with partial evidence; economics, rights, adoption or execution still uncertain.
- 4–6: generic opportunities or pilots without demonstrated advantage, funding or outcome measures.
- 0–3: no plausible fit, value likely captured by suppliers/competitors, or failed economics/control.
- N/A: evidence is insufficient to assess. Do not give high scores based on a general AI strategy or market hype.
Potential pathways include lower unit costs, better yield/quality, faster R&D, personalization, workflow integration, demand forecasting, fraud detection, service improvement, or new products. State where the benefit accrues: issuer, customer, supplier, platform, or model provider.

## Final trajectory classification
Apply these rules in order, only if composite coverage is at least 80%; otherwise report “Insufficient evidence — provisional”:
1. At Risk if current moat score <40/100, or residual AI disruption risk is High and mean trajectory ≤−1, or a sourced existential threat/failed response puts a core advantage under immediate threat.
2. Weakening if not At Risk and either mean trajectory ≤−0.5 or composite normalized score <50.
3. Strengthening if not At Risk/Weakening, mean trajectory ≥+0.5, composite normalized score ≥60, and residual AI risk is not High.
4. Stable in all other adequately evidenced cases.
These are decision rules for consistent labeling, not probability estimates. Explain overrides and uncertainty; do not claim stable means safe.

## Strategic inventory
Identify with evidence and separate labels:
- Moat sources and the mechanism that protects value.
- Moat threats, competitors and substitutes.
- AI disruption risks, exposure, residual risk and early-warning indicators.
- New moat opportunities and who captures value.
- Management actions already taken, planned actions, measurable milestones and actions that could strengthen the moat.
- Key uncertainties and events that would change the assessment.

## Bursa Malaysia application and safeguards
Use issuer filings and current Bursa announcements for Malaysian company facts; supplement with credible sector, customer, supplier, registry or regulator sources. Distinguish audited outcomes from management claims, binding contracts from plans, and disclosed from inferred market position. Adapt moat relevance to sector and business model; explain N/R choices. Check current PN/GN/listing status only when relevant and use current primary Bursa notices. Do not fabricate company evidence, imply unsupported market share, or give a buy/sell recommendation.
